## Persian Phonetic Legend

Persian orthography leaves short vowels unwritten and collapses English `/w/` and `/v/` into a single `و` letter, while collapsing dental fricatives `/θ/` and `/ð/` into `س` and `ز`. The Persian guide column below applies five explicit markers:

1. `ˈ` marks the primary stressed syllable immediately following it, while `·` separates spoken syllables.
2. `و(W)` indicates rounded-lip `/w/` formed by puckering both lips into an `oo` ring without touching the lower lip to the upper teeth.
3. `و(V)` indicates labiodental `/v/` formed by lightly touching the upper front teeth onto the lower lip with steady vocal vibration.
4. `ث(TH-بی‌صدا)` indicates unvoiced `/θ/` formed by placing the tongue tip gently between the front teeth and releasing soft air without vocal cord buzz.
5. `ذ(TH-صدادار)` indicates voiced `/ð/` formed in the same teeth-tongue position while buzzing the throat.
6. `ژ` marks `/ʒ/` (as in `ژاله`), heard in `Treasury` and `foreclosure`.

## Native Persian-to-English Interference Traps in Episode 01

### Trap 1: The /w/ Versus /v/ Collapse
Standard Persian uses a single letter `و` for both English `/w/` and `/v/`, which often leads speakers to substitute `/v/` in `Washington` (Line 19), `whiteboard` (Line 37), `wall` (`Wall Street`, Line 53), `wealth` (Line 53), `withholding` (Line 61), `Wielechowski` (Line 81), `West Virginia` (Line 101), and `gigawatt` (Line 109). Conversely, overcorrection can push `/v/` words toward `/w/` in `Silicon Valley` (Line 29), `Nvidia` (Line 59), `veto` (Line 79), `vanishes` (Line 95), and `equivalent` (Line 93). Look at Line 101 (`West Virginia`), where `/w/` and `/v/` stand side by side across a single space. Practice pushing both lips forward into an `oo` circle on `West`, then pulling your bottom lip straight onto your top teeth for `Virginia`.

### Trap 2: Initial /s/+Consonant Clusters Without an Added Vowel
Persian phonology forbids two consonants at the start of a syllable, so speakers reflexively insert a ghost `e-` vowel before English `s`-clusters. Episode 01 carries dense `s`-clusters right on accented camera beats: `spreadsheet` (Line 59), `scrip` (Line 101, 105), `stimulus` (Line 69), `statement` (`Monthly Treasury Statement`, Line 57), `standoff` (Line 83), `street` (`Wall Street`, Line 53), `stitched` (Line 35), `substations` (Line 111, 115), `spend` (`spend`, Line 103), and `State` (`Wielechowski v. State`, Line 81). Start every one of those words with a pure, unvoiced snake hiss (`ssss`) held for half a beat before dropping your tongue onto `/p/`, `/t/`, or `/k/`. Never let vocal cord vibration begin prior to the consonant release.

### Trap 3: Dental Fricatives /θ/ and /ð/ Turning Into /s/ and /z/
Saying `sirty` for `thirty` or `wizholding` for `withholding` immediately signals non-native articulation on close-up condenser mics. Episode 01 forces repeated dental transitions across fiscal numbers and nouns: `two-thirds` (Line 19), `twelve months` (Line 25), `thirty` (`thirty-year`, Line 37, 39; `thirty-two`, Line 39, 53), `wealth` (Line 53), `withholding` (Line 61), `thousand` (Line 79, 89, 103, 109), `trillion` (Lines 19, 23, 25, 83, 117), `through` (Lines 35, 61, 73, 123), `Three Mile Island` (Line 113), `thirteen` (Line 113), and `833 percent` (Line 113, 115). Note the special cluster trap in `months` (Lines 25, 51, 89, 115): place the tongue tip forward between your teeth for `/θ/` and slide it backward onto the ridge for `/s/` without dropping `/θ/`.

### Trap 4: Lax Versus Tense Vowels and Silent Letters
Persian `/iː/` is always long and tense, making short English `/ɪ/` drift toward `/iː/` in words like `stitched` (Line 35), `chips` (Lines 57, 59, 93), `still` (Lines 23, 25, 45, 59, 75, 123, 125), `sit` (Lines 43, 61, 109, 115), `ships` (Lines 71, 119), `split` (Line 123), and `hit` (Lines 25, 47, 69, 109, 113). Contrast those short lax vowels directly against tense `/iː/` words in the same script: `leave` (Line 103), `cheaper` (Lines 45, 93), `heating` (`heating-fuel`, Line 79), `seat` (Line 95), and `screen` (Line 129). Also lock down silent English letters present in Episode 01: silent `b` in `debt` (Lines 19, 25, 41, 63, 83, 125, 133), silent `t` in `mortgage` (Lines 23, 27, 39, 45, 53, 123), silent `g` in `sovereign` (Line 133) and `foreign` (Line 25), silent `s` in `Island` (Line 113), silent `w` in `underwriters` (Line 45), `wholesale` (Line 113), and `unwritten` (Line 127), plus silent `eau` collapse in `Juneau` (Line 83).

### Trap 5: Spoken Cadence Rules for Episode 01 Figures
Read multi-digit dollar figures and percentages in rhythmic trochees with tiny micro-pauses before unit nouns (`trillion`, `billion`, `percent`) so listeners process scale without strain. For `$40.5 trillion` (Line 19), read `FOR-tee point FYV / TRIL-yən`. For exact tax shares in Line 57 (`84.1 percent`, `10.8 percent`, `7.1 percent`), keep `point` dry and short, and strike `per-SENT` evenly. For four-digit housing and dividend numbers (`$3,400` in Line 39, `$2,052` and `$8,208` in Line 79), use `"thirty-four hundred"` for rounded `$3,400` to protect 150 WPM flow, while spelling out `"two thousand fifty-two"` and `"eight thousand two hundred eight"` when precision carries the legal weight of Alaska's dividend formula.
