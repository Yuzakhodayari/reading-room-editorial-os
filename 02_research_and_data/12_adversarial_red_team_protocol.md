# Deliverable 12: Adversarial red-team critique protocol and prompt pack

Most commentary channels covering artificial intelligence, government debt, corporate accounting, or cybersecurity build an argument by hiding the strongest objection in the room. They compare 2030 directly to the 1933 Great Depression without mentioning the gold standard, claim corporate tax write-offs make money evaporate from the economy, or pretend autonomous software conjures unpatched kernel zero-days out of thin air. Casual viewers may nod along for ten minutes, yet every bond strategist, tax partner, power-plant engineer, exploit researcher, and appellate lawyer watching spots the shortcut immediately and closes the tab.

*The Reading Room Editorial OS* runs the opposite play. Stage 2 of our mandatory six-stage script pipeline convenes five adversarial domain specialists to attack every research dossier (`Stage 1`) before structural outlining (`Stage 3`) begins. Whenever a specialist exposes an orthodox institutional defense, we bake that exact defense straight into Act I, Act II, or Act III spoken narration, grant what the specialist gets right, re-run our longitudinal household character's monthly budget inside that assumption, and prove how the trap still closes. Machine-readable audit gates for all five seats live inside `02_research_and_data/data/red_team_checklists.json`.

## 1. Stage 2 pipeline mechanics and the three-move steelman turn

No episode script or dossier (`ep_01` through `ep_30`) clears Stage 2 until all five specialist seats issue a written verdict against the Stage 1 primary evidence packet (`08_primary_institutional_source_directory.md` and `fact_ledger.json`). Every finding receives one of four severity codes:

1. `FATAL_THESIS_BREAK`: Primary ledger math, statutory text, or physical engineering contradicts the core episode premise. Production halts immediately; the pitch returns to Division 1 topic filtering or gets killed outright.
2. `MANDATORY_PROSE_CONCESSION`: An orthodox economist, CPA, grid planner, security engineer, or regulatory counsel has a legitimate counter-mechanism that alters how the shock transmits without saving the household balance sheet. The script must voice this counterargument explicitly in `45 to 75 spoken words` before delivering the narrower, harder failure mode.
3. `DENOMINATOR_PRECISION_FIX`: Units, accounting periods, procedural court postures, or cryptographic primitives carry technical ambiguity (`MW` mixed with `MWh`, `Gross Interest` mixed with `Net Interest`, `Rule 12(b)(6)` mixed with trial judgment). Fix the exact noun phrase and table crop before Stage 3.
4. `LEGAL_SAFE_HARBOR_GUARD`: Act IV personal protection steps stray too close to individualized investment, tax, or legal advice, or misstate statutory consumer liability limits. Insert clear balance-sheet framing and statutory boundaries.

### Spoken architecture: the Grant-Recalculate-Tighten turn

Amateur creators sound defensive when answering critics, leaning on throat-clearing clichés or negative-contrast formulas. *The Reading Room* voices every `MANDATORY_PROSE_CONCESSION` using a three-move turn modeled on *AI In Context* (`7x`), *Max Fisher* (`6x`), and *Patrick Boyle* deadpan precision:

1. Move 1 (`Grant the institutional mechanism`, `15 to 25 words`): Name the specialist's exact real-world rule in active, concrete language (`fiat 2 percent inflation targeting`, `taxable vendor revenue at chip fabs`, `Congress's sovereign power to issue dollars`, `fifteen-year power-plant construction incentives`).
2. Move 2 (`Recalculate the household ledger`, `15 to 25 words`): Apply that rule directly to our named character's Friday paycheck, `$3,400` fixed mortgage, `401(k)` penalty rule, electric bill, or checking account.
3. Move 3 (`Tighten the structural trap`, `15 to 25 words`): Show the specific timing gap, withholding asymmetry, statutory subordination, or physical bottleneck that crushes the household inside that exact orthodox world.

### Seat 1: Federal Reserve and sovereign macroeconomist

Seat 1 hunts down four macroeconomic fallacies that sink populist finance videos:

1. Fiat `2 percent` inflation targeting versus 1933 Gold Standard deflation. During the early 1930s, the US dollar was convertible into gold at `$20.67 per troy ounce`, constraining Federal Reserve liquidity while commercial bank failures shrank the money supply by nearly a third. Today's Federal Open Market Committee runs an unpegged fiat currency targeting `2 percent` annual Personal Consumption Expenditures (`PCE`) inflation. If AI unit-cost gains push general consumer prices downward, the Fed cuts `IORB` policy rates and expands `SOMA` securities holdings until aggregate prices keep rising near `2 percent`. Scripts must never assume economy-wide 1933 price deflation goes unanswered; the real household squeeze happens when monetary easing stops grocery bills from falling while automation collapses the relative market wage of human labor against fixed thirty-year mortgage debt.
2. Currency-issuing sovereign versus currency-using state or municipality. Alaska (`Juneau`), California, Detroit, and individual Eurozone member states are currency users that must tax or borrow external currency before spending. The consolidated US federal government (`Congress + Treasury + Federal Reserve`) issues the US dollar and can always credit nominal dollars to settle obligations. Whenever a script compares a federal transfer to a state budget crunch, Seat 1 requires an explicit fork: avoiding money creation traps citizen checks inside annual debt-ceiling fights subordinate to bond interest, while monetizing the gap avoids nominal default at the cost of bond-term-premium repricing and physical bottleneck inflation.
3. One-for-one paycheck replacement versus excess fiscal monetization. Paying a displaced worker `$4,000` in newly created dollars solely to replace `$4,000` of lost private wages leaves aggregate consumer demand roughly flat. Inflationary pressure erupts when Washington simultaneously monetizes a shrinking W-2 payroll tax base, `$2.1 trillion` in annual net bond interest, defense outlays, and tens of millions of monthly household checks while real energy, housing hookups, and copper remain supply-constrained.
4. `GDP = GDI` National Income and Product Accounts conservation. Every dollar saved by cutting W-2 payroll lands somewhere inside Gross Domestic Income (`corporate operating surplus`, `consumption of fixed capital`, `proprietors' income`, `net interest`) or passes to buyers via lower unit prices. Scripts must trace the distributional shift from labor share (`~53%`) into concentrated equity and depreciation pools (`Top 10%` holding `~90%` of stocks in `Fed DFA`) without claiming money disappeared from the national accounts.

### Seat 2: Forensic CPA and institutional credit analyst

Seat 2 audits every corporate tax, cash-flow, and shadow-credit claim against `IRC` statutes and `FASB ASC` standards:

1. B2B capital-expenditure buyer write-offs versus vendor taxable revenue. When Amazon or Microsoft deducts `$200 billion` of AI servers in year one under permanent `100 percent` bonus depreciation (`IRC § 168(k)`) and domestic R&D expensing (`IRC § 174A`), that cash enters the revenue line of Nvidia, semiconductor equipment makers, and electrical contractors. Seat 2 requires scripts to state that B2B spend stays inside the economy, then pinpoint the real fiscal leak: Friday W-2 paychecks suffer immediate FICA and income-tax withholding off the top before hitting a worker's bank, whereas corporate capital stacks upfront hardware deductions, R&D tax credits, `FDII` and foreign IP profit routing (`Ireland`, `Singapore`), and tax-free securities-backed borrowing by billionaire founders who never sell shares to trigger capital gains.
2. GAAP straight-line depreciation versus cash `CapEx` and `ASC 842` finance leases. Extending estimated server useful lives in `Form 10-K Note 1` from `3 years` to `5 or 6 years` lowers annual non-cash GAAP depreciation expense and flatters reported `Operating Income`, while doing nothing to slow cash leaving the treasury. Conversely, deducting `100 percent` of hardware on IRS tax returns while depreciating over six years on GAAP shareholder reports creates a deferred tax timing wedge. Always separate accrual `Net Income` from cash `Free Cash Flow` (`OCF - CapEx - Finance Lease Principal`), and check `ShareBasedCompensation` add-backs alongside `Form 8-K Item 2.03` off-balance-sheet GPU joint ventures.
3. Payment-in-Kind (`PIK`) non-cash interest versus cash default. Private-credit funds and Business Development Companies (`ASC 946`) routinely report `1 percent` headline cash default rates even when AI-disrupted enterprise software borrowers run out of cash. Inspecting loan-level `Schedules of Investments` reveals borrowers amending loan agreements to pay `SOFR + 600 bps` coupons in `PIK` notes. Under GAAP, the lender records non-cash `PIK` accruals as current `Investment Income` and pays out real cash dividends to fund investors, deferring the solvency reckoning until principal maturity.

### Seat 3: Power grid and industrial supply-chain engineer

Seat 3 enforces physical conservation laws, auction rules, and commodity midstream realities:

1. Short-run permitting and transformer queues versus ten-year price-elastic supply response. An `833 percent` jump in PJM wholesale capacity clearing prices (`$28.92` to `$269.92 per MW-day`) sends a loud market signal that attracts private investment into combined-cycle gas turbines, utility solar, grid batteries, and nuclear restarts over a ten-to-fifteen-year build cycle. Seat 3 bans claiming power supply stays frozen forever; the crisis sits inside the clock mismatch between `6-month` enterprise software deployment and `5-to-10-year` NEPA/FERC high-voltage transmission permitting plus `3-to-4-year` delivery backlogs for grain-oriented electrical steel (`GOES`) large power transformers (`LPTs`).
2. Nameplate `MW` versus capacity factor, `UCAP`, and `TWh`. Keep instantaneous power (`MW`, `GW`) strictly distinct from cumulative energy (`MWh`, `TWh`). Never compare `1,000 MW` of solar (`~24%` capacity factor) one-for-one against `1,000 MW` of `92%+` nuclear baseload (`Three Mile Island Unit 1`) when sizing `24/7` `8,760-hour` AI training campuses. Always filter speculative `EIA 860M` and `ERCOT GIS` interconnection queue totals for the `~70 to 80 percent` historical withdrawal rate.
3. Midstream refining versus raw mine extraction. Opening `USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries` and noting copper mines in Chile or Peru misses where physical bottlenecks close. Cross-check `IEA` midstream refining shares showing `45 to 98 percent` single-country concentration in chemical refining, gallium purification, rare-earth magnet separation, `GOES` transformer cores, and `HVDC` circuit breakers.

### Seat 4: Offensive security and cryptography researcher

Seat 4 eliminates Hollywood hacking mythology and enforces verifiable telemetry from `CISA`, `NIST`, `Verizon`, and `Mandiant`:

1. Disclosed `N-day` / `1-day` CVE weaponization versus `0-day` boundaries. Autonomous coding models do not need to invent unpatched `0-day` vulnerabilities out of thin air to break enterprise infrastructure. Over `95 percent` of confirmed intrusions in `Verizon DBIR` and `CISA KEV` exploit stolen credentials, OAuth session tokens, social-engineered push approvals, or disclosed `1-day` / `N-day` edge-appliance `CVEs` during the weeks between vendor patch release and enterprise deployment. Machine-speed scanning compresses the exploit window after patch Tuesday from weeks down to hours.
2. Kernel (`Ring 0`) versus user-space (`Ring 3`) and sandbox blast radius. Distinguish privileged kernel-mode drivers (`Ring 0`, where a faulty memory read crashes the host OS or bypasses endpoint telemetry entirely) from user-space browser agents, Docker containers, and cloud IAM roles (`Ring 3`). A prompt-injected browser assistant can drain authorized web sessions and API keys accessible to that user profile without touching the operating-system kernel.
3. Harvest-Now-Decrypt-Later (`HNDL`) secrecy shelf-life thresholds. Quantum Shor's algorithm breaks asymmetric public-key cryptography (`RSA-2048`, `ECDSA`, `ECDH`), whereas Grover's algorithm only halves effective symmetric key length, leaving `AES-256` data encryption secure. Under `NIST FIPS 203` (`ML-KEM`), `FIPS 204` (`ML-DSA`), `FIPS 205` (`SLH-DSA`), and `IR 8547` (`2030` deprecation, `2035` disallowance), `HNDL` interception matters today for TLS/VPN traffic protecting secrets with a ten-to-thirty-year shelf life (`sovereign intelligence`, `biometric/genomic records`, `chip lithography IP`, `long-lived root certificates`), while ephemeral `15-minute` login codes carry zero `HNDL` value by `2034`.

### Seat 5: Constitutional, antitrust, and tech-policy counsel

Seat 5 audits every statutory citation, appellate holding, and viewer-protection rule:

1. Annual statutory appropriation versus constitutional dedication lock. A benefit formula passed by ordinary legislation holds zero permanent shield against a future budget crisis. As the Alaska Supreme Court held unanimously in *Wielechowski v. State*, `403 P.3d 1141` (`Alaska 2017`), unless citizen payments sit inside an explicit constitutional dedication exempt from annual appropriation, lawmakers or a governor's veto can slash payouts whenever debt service and core agencies take priority.
2. Criminal `mens rea` intent versus civil strict liability and `FTC Section 5`. Criminal prosecution of corporate officers requires proving statutory `mens rea` (`knowing`, `willful`, or `reckless` state of mind beyond a reasonable doubt). When autonomous pricing, lending, or medical agents cause harm without explicit human criminal intent, legal exposure lands inside civil product-design defect law, principal-agent contract liability, `DOJ Sherman Act` algorithmic collusion injunctions, and `FTC Section 5` (`15 U.S.C. § 45`) unfair or deceptive practice remedies.
3. `47 U.S.C. § 230` third-party speech immunity versus first-party product design and payment statutes. `Section 230(c)(1)` shields platforms when acting as publisher or speaker of third-party user content; it does not immunize first-party hardware defects, employment wage violations under the `Fair Labor Standards Act` (`29 U.S.C. § 203(m)` and `29 CFR § 531.34` barring non-negotiable company scrip), or commercial-bank unauthorized transfer liability under `Regulation E` (`12 CFR Part 1005`, which protects consumers against unauthorized account hacks while leaving victims on the hook when social engineering tricks them into authorizing a wire or push payment themselves).
4. `31 U.S.C. § 5318(g)(2)` Bank Secrecy Act anti-tipping-off rules and Act IV non-advisory guardrails. When automated bank compliance models freeze a depositor's checking account and mail a closure check weeks later with zero explanation, bank staff stay silent because `31 U.S.C. § 5318(g)(2)` and `31 CFR § 1020.320(e)` make disclosing a `FinCEN Suspicious Activity Report` (`SAR`) to the customer a federal crime. Finally, every script touching personal debt, equity, taxes, or legal rights must include an explicit non-advisory disclosure in Act IV (`None of this is financial or legal advice`) and frame takeaways as structural balance-sheet positioning.

### Line-by-line surgical breakdown of all four Episode 01 patches
