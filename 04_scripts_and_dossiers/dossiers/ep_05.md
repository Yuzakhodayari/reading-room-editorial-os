# Episode 05 Dossier: The $2.5T Shadow Debt Behind AI SaaS

## 1. Structural Thesis and 2027-to-2036 Horizon Immunity

Wall Street's `$2.5 trillion` private credit market lent roughly `$500 billion` to private-equity enterprise software buyouts (`FL-062`) on a single underwriting rule: corporate per-seat software subscriptions never shrink. As autonomous workflow agents and `>99%` inference token deflation (`FL-034`) let corporate buyers replace five hundred human CRM, HR, and ticketing seats with lean API orchestrators, debt-financed SaaS borrowers run short of cash to service floating-rate debt. To avoid reporting headline cash defaults, lenders and sponsors use Payment-in-Kind (`PIK`) toggles and maturity extensions in `88%` of distressed workouts (`FL-063`), folding unpaid interest onto loan principal while Business Development Companies (`BDCs`) keep booking paper income.

This dossier holds structural immunity across 2027 through 2036 because direct-lending buyout facilities carry five-to-seven-year maturities with amend-and-extend walls clustering between 2028 and 2033. Every enterprise seat-license trim or quarterly `SEC Form 10-Q Schedule of Investments` filing widens the wedge between accrued GAAP `PIK` income and cash recovery.
